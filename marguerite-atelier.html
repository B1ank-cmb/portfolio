Statement

I am not interested in objects that look easy to make.

Every piece carries the record of its own making — the pull of the wheel, the place where a thumb pressed too hard, the way ash settled unevenly in the kiln. Industrial ceramics work to erase these. I work to keep them.

The glazes come from material gathered within a hundred miles of the studio: wood ash from a neighbor's stove, iron-rich clay from a creek bed two towns over, feldspar from a quarry that has been closed since 1974. This means no two firings are identical and roughly a third of what I make does not survive. That ratio is not a problem to be solved. It is the cost of the surface.

A vessel should be worth picking up. Everything else is secondary.
